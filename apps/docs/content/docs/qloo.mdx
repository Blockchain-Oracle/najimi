Najimi would not work without [Qloo](https://www.qloo.com). Qloo's taste graph knows how the things people love connect across kinds: someone who loves *Insecure* and *Americanah* tends to love Jorja Smith, a certain kind of restaurant, a certain kind of room. Every screen in Najimi is a Qloo answer, seen a different way.

## The calls

| Endpoint | What Najimi asks it |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /search` | Turn a name into a Qloo id: the loves you pick, cities, a business near its address, the "pick three" grid. |
| `GET /entities` | Match your imports exactly: X and Instagram handles, Goodreads and MusicBrainz ids. |
| `POST /v2/insights` (weighted) | Your picks of every kind, with your loves as `{ id, weight }` and **explainability** on. |
| `/v2/insights` `urn:tag` | Your taste's words: aesthetics, decor, ambience, style, cuisine, dishes. |
| `/v2/insights` `urn:heatmap` | Where in a city a taste lives, square by square (Earth, trips, business). |
| `/v2/insights` `urn:demographics` | Who a taste leans to, by age and gender (business plans, the agent). |
| `/v2/insights` with `signal.location` | A spot's own scene, with no person in the signal: its artists, brands, places and words. |
| `/v2/insights` with `filter.results.entities` | How well a shortlist fits someone: games, group fit, feed scores. |
| `GET /v2/analysis/compare` | What two tastes share: your match with anyone, a business against its street. |
| `GET /v2/tags` | Find tags by meaning ("candlelit", "jazz bar", a business's kind). |
| `GET /v2/audiences` | Audiences the agent can lean a taste with. |

| **Feed** | The first page is your stored picks; every page after asks Qloo for the next page of the same weighted signal. Loving a card adds it to your taste, and the next reveal reshapes the feed. |
| **More like this** | Your taste with that pick added at full weight. |

| **Graph** | Grow a node: insights seeded by that one thing, for its own kind and one other. |
| **Match** | `compare` on both people's loves: shared words with scores, and what's only theirs. |
| **Games** | *Guess my wavelength* and *Blind Taste* score a shortlist against the subject's taste with `filter.results.entities`. Qloo's answer is hidden until the reveal. |
| **Group fit** | Picks from the circle's blended taste, then each member's own fit for every pick. |
| **Night plans** | For each stop (drinks, food, late), place insights around the spot you named, then each person's fit. |
| **Trips** | The city's heatmap names the areas to stay in, one per day; morning, afternoon and evening stops come from place insights around each. |
| **Business** | About 17 calls: find the business near its address (or the places most like it), what its fans love across seven kinds, their words and lean, where they cluster, the street's own scene, what's only yours versus only the street's, and places nearby with rising `audience_growth`. |
| **Your feeds (daily follows)** | People, artists and brands for your taste, with the X and Instagram handles Qloo carries; TikTok and YouTube through the Wikidata ids Qloo returns. |
| **Imports** | Handles and ids go to `/entities` first (exact), the rest to `/search` by name. |
| **Feed score** | Names read from your screenshot, matched in Qloo, then scored against your taste; a fit of 0.8 or more counts as "you". |

Twins, the radar and a circle's overlap are worked out from the Qloo picks already stored for each person, with no new call.

## The agent's Qloo tools

Najimi gets Qloo's whole surface as tools, not a narrow "recommend" button, and composes them for each ask.

| Tool | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| `qlooSearch` | Turn a name ("Fela", "Kyoto") into an id to use as a signal, filter or comparison. |
| `qlooTags` | Find tags by meaning: moods, genres, cuisines, decor, kinds of place. |
| `qlooInsights` | Anything the graph answers: picks of any kind, words, a heatmap, a demographic lean. Every documented signal and filter: location, tags, audiences, age and gender, popularity, release years, ratings, price, opening hours, external ids, `diversify.by`. |
| `qlooCompare` | Two tastes side by side. |
| `qlooAudiences` | Audiences to lean a taste with. |

Your loves are the default signal, and the agent adds to them:

| Ask | How it's composed |
| --- | --- |
| "70s films I'd love" | `movie`, release years 1970–1979 |
| "Hidden gems" | popularity at most 0.3 |
| "Where would I hang out in Paris?" | a heatmap of Paris, with its areas named |
| "Is Kendrick a fit for me?" | your taste, scored against his id |
| "A trip around my taste" | `destination`, one per city |
| "Music for this room" | `qlooTags "candlelit"` as the signal |

Every recommendation the agent makes stands on a Qloo result in the chat. If Qloo has nothing, it says so.

## Why: explainability

Every weighted request asks Qloo which love pulled each result in, and by how much. That share is the **because** chip on every pick ("because you love Fleabag"), the **Made of your loves** bar when you open one, and the line under your taste ("Fleabag led to Billie Eilish"). When you drag a love's weight on the Graph, the shares re-rank everything at once, before Qloo refines it.

## Seeing every call

Every Qloo request is logged: path, parameters, status, time, cache hit and result count (the request only; Qloo's answer never leaves the server).

- The <QlooLogo /> chip on a card in the chat opens the exact request behind it.
- A **because** chip opens the call that pick came from.
- **Built on <QlooLogo />** on your taste lists every call the reveal made, in order.
- A business plan ends with how many Qloo calls built it.

## Fast and fair

- **One pipe.** Every call goes through one client: at most four at a time, three tries on 429 and 5xx with backoff (honouring `Retry-After`), a 20-second timeout.
- **Cached by meaning.** Answers are kept in Postgres under a key made of the path, the parameters and the body: 30 days for search, entities, tags and audiences, 7 days for insights and compare. Identical requests in flight share one call, so a reload or a retry costs Qloo nothing.
- **Nothing public.** Qloo's data stays in the database; the public repository holds none of it.
